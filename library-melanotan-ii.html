2.Why researchers choose it

Melanotan II (MT-II) was designed by molecular dynamics from the α-MSH(4–10) core, His-Phe-Arg-Trp, with norleucine replacing methionine, D-phenylalanine at the position that controls receptor recognition, and a lactam bridge between aspartate and lysine that locks the active turn. The result is a heptapeptide that is far more potent and more stable than the parent hormone.

It activates the MC1, MC3, MC4 and MC5 receptors but not MC2, so it serves as the general melanocortin agonist against which selective ligands are measured, and it is the usual agonist in experiments that use the antagonist SHU9119 to confirm receptor involvement.

3.What the research has found

In the original design study, the cyclic lactam analogues showed potent and prolonged activity in the frog skin and lizard skin pigment-dispersion bioassays compared with α-MSH, confirming the active conformation of the core sequence. [1]

Radioligand binding at the cloned human melanocortin receptors defined its affinity profile across subtypes and established it as a non-selective agonist. [2]

Given centrally to mice, MT-II suppressed feeding and the effect was blocked by SHU9119 and absent in the agouti obesity model, work that placed the MC4 receptor in the control of energy balance and made MT-II a standard tool in hypothalamic circuit studies. [3]

4.Applications at the bench

Binding and cAMP assays at melanocortin receptor subtypes; pigment-dispersion bioassays; melanocyte and B16 melanoma culture for melanogenesis and tyrosinase induction; rodent studies of feeding, energy expenditure and autonomic outflow; and comparator work with selective MC4 receptor agonists and antagonists.

5.Handling notes

Store the lyophilised powder at −20 °C, desiccated and protected from light, as the tryptophan is light-sensitive. It dissolves readily in water; a 1 mg/mL stock is typical. The single tryptophan gives ε ≈ 5,500 M⁻¹ cm⁻¹ at 280 nm. The cyclic structure and D-phenylalanine make it stable in serum-containing media for the length of most assays.
