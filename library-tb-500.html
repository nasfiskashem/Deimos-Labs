What it is

Thymosin β4 is a 43-residue protein found in nearly all mammalian cells, where it is the main actin-sequestering peptide. It binds monomeric G-actin and controls the pool available for polymerisation. The segment spanning residues 17 to 23 (Leu-Lys-Lys-Thr-Glu-Thr-Gln) is the core of that actin-binding site.

The research product usually called TB-500 is this seven-residue fragment, normally with an N-terminal acetyl group. It is not the full-length thymosin β4 protein, and results obtained with one should not be assumed to apply to the other.

Where it is used in research

The fragment is used to probe actin binding and to compare the activity of the short motif with the full-length protein in cell migration, adhesion and wound-closure assays. Full-length thymosin β4 has been studied in preclinical models of cardiac, corneal and dermal repair, and the 17–23 fragment is often included in those studies as a comparator.

Because the sequence is short, it is also a convenient model peptide for stability and peptidase assays.

Handling notes

Store at −20 °C, desiccated. The peptide is freely soluble in water. Aliquot reconstituted stock and store frozen; use within a month for best results.

There are no aromatic residues, so quantify by HPLC at 214 nm or by weight.

Regulatory status

Thymosin β4 and its fragments are not approved medicines and appear on the World Anti-Doping Agency prohibited list. Supplied for laboratory research only.
