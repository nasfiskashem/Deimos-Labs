2.Why researchers choose it

Thymosin β4 is the body's main actin-sequestering protein and a key regulator of cell migration. The segment spanning residues 17 to 23 (LKKTETQ) is its actin-binding motif, and this short fragment has been shown to reproduce the actin binding and cell-migration activity of the full protein.

At seven residues it is inexpensive, easy to handle and highly reproducible, which makes it the practical choice for labs that want the activity of thymosin β4 without the cost of the 43-residue protein.

3.What the research has found

Thymosin β4 and its 17–23 fragment have been reported to promote endothelial and keratinocyte migration, speed closure in scratch-wound and dermal models, and to change inflammatory markers at the injury site. [1]

In cardiac research, thymosin β4 has been shown to activate epicardial progenitor cells, encourage new vessel growth and improve cardiac function after experimental infarction. In corneal epithelial models it has been reported to promote cell migration. [2]

Work on the fragment itself demonstrates that the actin-binding motif is sufficient for the migration and wound-closure effects, which is why it is so widely used as the active unit. [3]

4.Applications at the bench

Cell migration, adhesion and scratch-wound assays; actin polymerisation and binding studies; dermal, corneal and cardiac injury models; angiogenesis assays; and peptidase stability work where a short, well-characterised sequence is needed.

5.Handling notes

Store at −20 °C, desiccated. Freely soluble in water. Aliquot reconstituted stock and store frozen; use within a month. Quantify by HPLC at 214 nm or by weight, as there are no aromatic residues.
