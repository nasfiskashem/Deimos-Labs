What it is

Angiotensin II is the octapeptide Asp-Arg-Val-Tyr-Ile-His-Pro-Phe. It is produced from angiotensin I by angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) and acts through two G-protein-coupled receptors, AT1 and AT2. The AT1 receptor mediates most of its classical effects on vascular tone, aldosterone release and sodium handling.

Human, bovine and rat angiotensin II share the same sequence, so a single product serves most mammalian models.

Where it is used in research

It is a reference agonist in receptor binding, calcium-flux and reporter assays, and is used to induce contraction in isolated vessel preparations. Chronic infusion by osmotic minipump is a standard rodent model of hypertension, cardiac hypertrophy and renal fibrosis.

It is also a substrate and reference compound in assays of angiotensinases and in mass-spectrometry method development.

Handling notes

Store at −20 °C, desiccated. Soluble in water to at least 1 mg/mL; acidify slightly if the solution is cloudy. The tyrosine residue allows quantification by absorbance at 280 nm. Aliquot and freeze; avoid repeated freeze–thaw cycles.

Angiotensin II is sensitive to peptidases in serum and tissue homogenates, so include protease inhibitors where relevant.
