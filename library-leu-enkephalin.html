2.Why researchers choose it

Leu-enkephalin (YGGFL) was identified in 1975 as one of the brain's own opioid peptides, a discovery that reshaped pain research. It remains the standard natural agonist for the delta opioid receptor, with useful activity at mu receptors as well.

It is small, highly pure, inexpensive and exceptionally well characterised, which makes it the first choice for receptor assays, peptidase studies and analytical method development.

3.What the research has found

Enkephalin signalling has been shown to modulate pain transmission, reward, stress responses and gastrointestinal function, and delta receptor activation is an active area of research for analgesia with a better side-effect profile than classical opioids. [1]

Because the peptide is rapidly cleared by aminopeptidase N and neprilysin, it has been central to the development of enkephalinase inhibitors, a drug class that boosts the body's own opioid signalling and has reached clinical testing for pain and depression. [2]

4.Applications at the bench

Delta and mu opioid receptor binding and functional assays; peptidase activity and inhibitor screening; microdialysis and neurochemical analysis; mass-spectrometry reference standard; and teaching and method-development work.

5.Handling notes

Store at −20 °C, desiccated. Soluble in water and dilute acetic acid. Quantify at 280 nm using the tyrosine residue (ε ≈ 1,280 M⁻¹cm⁻¹) or by HPLC. Prepare fresh working solutions for enzyme-containing systems.
