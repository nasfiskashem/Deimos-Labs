Amyloid β (1–42)

The 42-residue amyloid β peptide, used in aggregation, toxicity and immunoassay work in Alzheimer's disease research.

What it is

Amyloid β peptides are produced when the amyloid precursor protein (APP) is cleaved by β- and γ-secretase. The 1–42 form is more hydrophobic and aggregates faster than the more abundant 1–40 form, and it is the main constituent of the amyloid plaques seen in Alzheimer's disease.

The peptide readily forms soluble oligomers, protofibrils and mature fibrils in solution, and the behaviour of a given preparation depends heavily on how it was prepared.

Where it is used in research

Aβ 1–42 is used in thioflavin T aggregation kinetics, fibril and oligomer preparation, electron microscopy and atomic force microscopy of amyloid structure, neurotoxicity assays in cultured neurones and as a calibrator in immunoassays. It is also a common target in screens for aggregation inhibitors and in antibody development.

Handling notes

Store the lyophilised powder at −20 °C or below, desiccated. Because the peptide aggregates during dissolution, most protocols first dissolve it in hexafluoroisopropanol (HFIP), evaporate to a film and then resuspend in DMSO before diluting into buffer. This gives a monomeric starting material and reproducible aggregation.

Work quickly, keep solutions cold and avoid repeated freeze–thaw. Use low-binding tubes and tips, because the peptide adsorbs to plastic. Quantify by absorbance at 280 nm (one tyrosine) or by amino-acid analysis for accurate work.
