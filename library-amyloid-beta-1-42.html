Why researchers choose it

Amyloid β 1–42 is the main constituent of the amyloid plaques that characterise Alzheimer's disease, and the form most strongly linked to the disease process. Any lab working on amyloid aggregation, neurotoxicity, diagnostics or anti-amyloid therapeutics needs a reliable, high-purity source of it.

Purity and batch consistency matter more for Aβ 1–42 than for almost any other peptide, because trace impurities and pre-formed aggregates change its behaviour. Our batches ship with HPLC and mass spectrometry data so you can start from a known state.

What the research has found

Aβ 1–42 is the central molecule of the amyloid hypothesis, which has guided Alzheimer's research for more than thirty years and underpins the anti-amyloid antibodies now approved for early disease. Soluble oligomers of the peptide have been shown to impair synaptic function and memory in animal models, and fibril formation is the basis of thioflavin T assays used to screen aggregation inhibitors.

The peptide is also the calibrator for cerebrospinal fluid and plasma immunoassays, the target of most amyloid-imaging agents and a model system for amyloid formation more generally.

Applications at the bench

Thioflavin T aggregation kinetics; oligomer and fibril preparation; electron and atomic force microscopy of amyloid structure; neurotoxicity assays in cultured neurones; immunoassay calibration; antibody development; and screening of aggregation inhibitors.

Handling notes

Store at −20 °C or below, desiccated. To start from monomer, dissolve in hexafluoroisopropanol (HFIP), evaporate to a film and resuspend in DMSO before diluting into buffer. Work quickly, keep solutions cold, use low-binding plasticware and avoid repeated freeze–thaw. Quantify at 280 nm (one tyrosine) or by amino-acid analysis.
