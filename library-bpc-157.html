Why researchers choose it

BPC-157 is a synthetic 15-residue peptide derived from a protective protein in human gastric juice. It is unusually robust for a peptide of its size: stable in water for days at room temperature and resistant to gastric acid, which gives it a practical advantage in oral and long-duration experiments where most peptides break down.

Over three decades of published work have built a broad evidence base across tissue types, making it a well-mapped tool for repair and regeneration research.

What the research has found

In rodent models, BPC-157 has been reported to accelerate healing of transected Achilles tendon, medial collateral ligament, crushed muscle and segmental bone defects, with improved biomechanical and histological outcomes compared with controls.

In the gut, it has been shown to protect against and speed recovery from lesions caused by NSAIDs, alcohol and ischaemia, and to support healing of anastomoses and fistulas. Vascular studies report rapid recruitment of collateral circulation after major vessel occlusion.

Mechanistic work links these effects to the nitric oxide system, VEGF and VEGFR2 signalling, early growth response gene 1 (Egr-1) expression and enhanced fibroblast and endothelial cell migration, which together point to a coordinated angiogenic and cytoprotective action.

Applications at the bench

Tendon, ligament, muscle and bone healing models; gastrointestinal cytoprotection and ulcer studies; vascular occlusion and angiogenesis assays; fibroblast migration and growth-factor signalling in culture; and behavioural models exploring interactions with the dopamine and serotonin systems.

Handling notes

Store the lyophilised powder at −20 °C, desiccated. Reconstitute in sterile water or saline; a 1 mg/mL stock is typical. Aliquot after reconstitution and avoid repeated freeze–thaw cycles.

BPC-157 has no aromatic residues, so quantify by weight or by HPLC at 214 nm rather than by absorbance at 280 nm.
