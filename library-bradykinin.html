Why researchers choose it

Bradykinin (RPPGFSPFR) is released from kininogen by kallikrein and is the natural agonist of the B2 receptor. It gives fast, potent and highly reproducible responses in endothelial, smooth muscle and sensory neurone preparations, making it a dependable positive control.

It is also one of the best-behaved peptides in mass spectrometry, ionising cleanly with a well-characterised fragmentation pattern, which is why it appears in so many method-development and calibration protocols.

What the research has found

Bradykinin is a potent vasodilator acting through endothelial nitric oxide and prostacyclin release, and its signalling is central to research on blood pressure regulation, vascular permeability, inflammation and nociception.

Its degradation by ACE explains a large part of the cardiovascular benefit of ACE inhibitors, and research on its receptors has produced licensed treatments for hereditary angioedema. Kinin research continues to inform work on cardioprotection, kidney function and ischaemic preconditioning.

Applications at the bench

B2 receptor binding and signalling assays; calcium imaging and phosphoinositide turnover; endothelial nitric oxide release; isolated vessel and ileum preparations; pain and inflammation models; ACE and neprilysin activity assays; and mass-spectrometry calibration.

Handling notes

Store at −20 °C, desiccated. Freely soluble in water. Prepare fresh dilutions for assays, since serum enzymes degrade it quickly. Quantify at 257 nm (two phenylalanines) or by HPLC.
