2.Why researchers choose it

α-Melanocyte-stimulating hormone does two unrelated things in experimental systems: it drives pigmentation through the MC1 receptor and it suppresses inflammatory signalling. The lysyl-prolyl-valine tripeptide at its C-terminus retains much of the second activity with little of the first, so it lets a laboratory study the anti-inflammatory arm on its own.

At 342 g/mol it is small, stable, highly water-soluble and inexpensive to use at the concentrations that cell and tissue models require.

3.What the research has found

In the original fragment studies, KPV reduced experimentally induced inflammation in rodents in a way that paralleled the parent hormone, which established the C-terminal tripeptide as the minimal anti-inflammatory motif of α-MSH. [1]

In intestinal epithelial cells and immune cells, KPV lowers NF-κB activation and the release of pro-inflammatory cytokines such as IL-6, IL-8 and TNF-α. In mouse models of colitis, it reduces disease scores and inflammatory markers, and its uptake into epithelial cells is mediated by the di/tripeptide transporter PepT1, which is itself upregulated in inflamed tissue. [2]

Reviews of the melanocortin-derived tripeptides place these effects alongside those of α-MSH in keratinocytes, fibroblasts, mast cells and endothelium, and note that some of the activity is receptor-independent. [3]

4.Applications at the bench

NF-κB reporter and cytokine release assays; models of intestinal inflammation and barrier function; studies of PepT1 transport and peptide uptake; comparison with α-MSH and other melanocortin fragments in keratinocyte and macrophage culture; and as a small, well-defined standard in peptide transport and peptidase assays.

5.Handling notes

Store the lyophilised powder at −20 °C, desiccated. KPV is freely soluble in water and buffer at 10 mg/mL and above. Prepare working solutions fresh or store aliquots frozen.

It has no aromatic residues and no absorbance at 280 nm, so quantify by weight or by HPLC at 214 nm. The N-terminal lysine and C-terminal carboxylate make it a substrate for aminopeptidases and carboxypeptidases in serum-containing media, which should be allowed for in the design of longer incubations.
