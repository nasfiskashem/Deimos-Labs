2.Why researchers choose it

Tirzepatide was built on the glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide (GIP) sequence and engineered to engage the GLP-1 receptor as well. Two α-aminoisobutyric acid residues (positions 2 and 13) block dipeptidyl peptidase IV and stiffen the helix, and a C20 fatty diacid attached to lysine 20 through a γ-glutamate and two AEEA spacers binds albumin, which is what gives the molecule its long residence time in animals.

Because its two activities are carried by one molecule with known structure, it is the natural reference compound for any experiment that asks how GIP receptor and GLP-1 receptor signalling interact, and it is widely used as a positive control alongside receptor-selective agonists and antagonists.

3.What the research has found

In the discovery work, tirzepatide bound the GIP receptor with affinity comparable to native GIP and the GLP-1 receptor several-fold more weakly than native GLP-1, and in rodent models of obesity and diabetes it lowered glucose and body weight more than a matched GLP-1 receptor agonist. [1]

Detailed pharmacology showed that the molecule is imbalanced and biased: it is a full agonist at the GIP receptor, while at the GLP-1 receptor it favours cAMP production over β-arrestin recruitment and causes less receptor internalisation than GLP-1 itself. Cryo-electron microscopy structures of tirzepatide bound to each receptor explain how one sequence fits both binding pockets. [2]

Mechanistic studies in obese mice attribute part of its effect on insulin sensitivity to GIP receptor agonism that is independent of body-weight change, with changes in adipose tissue and islet function. [3]

4.Applications at the bench

Receptor binding, cAMP accumulation and β-arrestin recruitment assays in GIPR- and GLP-1R-expressing cell lines; internalisation and trafficking studies; islet perifusion and adipocyte culture; rodent studies of glucose handling and energy balance; structural biology of class B GPCRs; and use as a reference standard in LC-MS and immunoassays.

5.Handling notes

Store the lyophilised powder at −20 °C, desiccated. The lipidated, amphipathic sequence aggregates readily in acidic solution, so dissolve it in water or a mildly basic buffer (pH 7.5–8) at low concentration, swirl rather than vortex, filter, aliquot and freeze. One tryptophan and two tyrosines give ε ≈ 8,480 M⁻¹ cm⁻¹ at 280 nm.
