    How the numbers are worked out

    Concentration is peptide mass divided by diluent volume: 5 mg in 2 mL is 2.5 mg/mL, which is the same as 2.5 µg/µL. Molar concentration is concentration divided by molecular weight: 2.5 g/L ÷ 1419.55 g/mol = 1.76 mmol/L. The volume that contains a given amount is amount divided by concentration: 250 µg at 2.5 µg/µL is 100 µL. The dry powder's own volume is treated as negligible, and the number of samples is the total volume divided by the sample volume, rounded down.

    Syringe graduations

    A 1 mL graduated syringe is commonly marked in 100 divisions, so one division is 10 µL (0.01 mL). The reading is that conversion applied to the sample volume and nothing more: 100 µL is 10 divisions whatever the solution contains, and a 0.5 mL syringe with 50 divisions uses the same 0.01 mL spacing, so the drawing can be set to match the syringe in your laboratory. A reading on a syringe scale is a volume. It is not an instruction to use a syringe, and it says nothing about how, where or whether any solution is used.

    What it does not do

    It does not choose a peptide, an amount, a concentration, a solvent or a piece of equipment, and it has no defaults for any of them. It does not allow for net peptide content, counter-ion mass, losses on transfer, or the accuracy of your balance and pipettes. Treat its output as a check on your own calculation, not a replacement for it, and check it against your laboratory's procedures.
