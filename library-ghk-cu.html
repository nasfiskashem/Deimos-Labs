What it is

GHK is the tripeptide glycyl-L-histidyl-L-lysine. It was first isolated from human plasma in 1973 and binds copper(II) ions through the histidine imidazole and the backbone amide nitrogens, forming the complex known as GHK-Cu. Plasma levels of GHK fall markedly with age, which prompted much of the early interest in it.

The complex has a characteristic blue-violet colour in solution. The copper-free peptide is colourless.

Where it is used in research

GHK-Cu is used in fibroblast and keratinocyte culture to study collagen and glycosaminoglycan synthesis, matrix metalloproteinase expression and cell migration. It is also used as a model compound for copper delivery and copper-dependent enzyme studies.

Gene-expression profiling studies have reported that GHK alters the expression of a large number of human genes in cultured cells, which has made it a frequent reference compound in dermatological and wound-model research.

Handling notes

Store at −20 °C, desiccated and protected from light. Dissolve in water; avoid phosphate buffers at high concentration, which can precipitate copper. Reconstituted solutions are best used within a week when refrigerated.

The molecular weight quoted on our specification is for the peptide. The copper complex adds approximately 63.5 g/mol.
