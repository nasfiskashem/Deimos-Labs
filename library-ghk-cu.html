Why researchers choose it

GHK (glycyl-L-histidyl-L-lysine) is found in human plasma, saliva and urine, and binds copper(II) with high affinity to form GHK-Cu. Plasma levels fall sharply with age, from around 200 ng/mL at 20 to 80 ng/mL at 60, which has driven decades of research into its role in tissue maintenance.

Its activity in cell culture is well documented at nanomolar concentrations, and it is among the most cited peptides in dermatological and wound-model research.

What the research has found

In fibroblast culture, GHK-Cu has been shown to stimulate synthesis of collagen, elastin, glycosaminoglycans and decorin, and to increase production of the matrix remodelling enzymes and their inhibitors in a balanced way that favours healthy tissue.

Wound models report faster closure, improved blood vessel growth and increased recruitment of immune and repair cells. In hair follicle studies it has been shown to enlarge follicles and extend the growth phase.

A landmark gene-expression analysis found that GHK modulates the expression of around a third of human genes, resetting many towards a younger, healthier pattern. These data have made it a reference compound in studies of skin ageing, wound healing, lung tissue repair and neuroprotection.

Applications at the bench

Fibroblast and keratinocyte culture; collagen and matrix synthesis assays; wound-healing and angiogenesis models; hair follicle studies; gene-expression profiling; and copper transport and copper-dependent enzyme studies.

Handling notes

Store at −20 °C, desiccated and protected from light. Dissolve in water; avoid concentrated phosphate buffers, which can precipitate copper. Reconstituted solutions are best used within a week when refrigerated. The quoted molecular weight is for the peptide; the copper complex adds approximately 63.5 g/mol.
